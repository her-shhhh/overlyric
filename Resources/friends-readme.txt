Overlyric {{VERSION}} - sing along to anything on Spotify


HOW THIS HAPPENED

I always have music on while I work, and a song is twice as good when
I can sing along. These days most of my singing happens between Claude
sessions: Claude writes the code, I perform for an audience of zero.
The problem? I never know the words. So it's confident humming and the
odd made-up line. So I asked Claude to fix that too, and it built this.
Yes, Claude built its own karaoke machine for the breaks it gives me.
Now the lyrics float right on my screen, and the breaks are louder.


WHAT IT IS

A tiny Mac app that floats the lyrics of whatever Spotify is playing
on top of everything you do: big, clean, Instagram-story style, and
perfectly in time. Only the words show up; the rest is see-through,
so your work stays visible (your excuses for not working: also
visible). No login, no account, no subscription, no nonsense.

You need: macOS {{MIN_OS}} or later, the Spotify desktop app, and the
courage to sing in front of your colleagues.


SET IT UP (2 minutes, once)

1. Drag Overlyric onto the Applications folder in this window.
   Do NOT double-click it in here. macOS won't remember that you said
   yes for an app opened inside this window, and will keep asking.
   Forever. Like a song stuck on repeat, but worse.

2. Open Overlyric from your Applications folder. The first time, macOS
   panics with a dramatic warning (see "About the scary warnings"
   below - it's fine, it says that about everyone):
   - macOS 15 or later: click Done, then go to System Settings >
     Privacy & Security, scroll to the bottom, click Open Anyway next
     to Overlyric (within an hour) and confirm with your password.
   - macOS 14: Control-click Overlyric, choose Open, then Open again.

3. Find the little microphone in your menu bar (top right). That's
   the control room: every setting lives there, and there's no Dock
   icon. If your menu bar is a crowded party and the mic is hiding
   behind the notch, just right-click the lyrics - same menu.

4. Play a song in Spotify. When macOS asks if Overlyric may control
   Spotify, click Allow. (It only wants to know what you're playing.
   Judgement-free. Mostly.)


USE IT

- Drag the lyrics anywhere you like. Hold Cmd and scroll on them to
  make them bigger (for the chorus) or smaller (for meetings).
- Click the lyrics to jump to Spotify. Right-click them for the menu.
- Lyrics Style: 10 looks - Typewriter, Karaoke, Pop, Cube and more.
  Try them all. Pick a favourite. Change your mind every song.
- Lyrics Colour: pick one, or Auto (a fresh colour you can read on
  whatever is behind the lyrics), or Match album artwork.
- There are a few easter eggs hidden in here. You'll find them.
  Probably. Sing more and they find you.


ABOUT THE SCARY WARNINGS (why they appear, and why it's fine)

- "Apple could not verify Overlyric is free of malware": macOS says
  this about every app that isn't from the App Store or from a
  developer in Apple's paid ($99/year) programme. It is not a scan
  result - Apple simply hasn't looked at it. You approve it once.
- "Overlyric wants access to control Spotify": macOS words this very
  dramatically for any app that talks to another app. Overlyric only
  asks Spotify which song is playing and how far in it is. It never
  touches your account, playlists or likes. (One exception: if you
  click the "encore?" offer at the end of a song, it plays that song
  again. You asked for it.)
- "Screen Recording" (only if you turn on Lyrics Colour > Auto): it
  peeks at a tiny patch right behind the lyrics to pick a colour you
  can read. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere. The purple dot in the
  menu bar is just macOS telling on it.
- It talks to exactly two places: lrclib.net (a free, community-made
  lyrics site) for the lyrics, and Spotify's image server for the
  album cover if you pick Match album artwork. No tracking, no ads,
  no "we value your privacy" pop-ups.
- To remove it: quit it from the microphone menu, then drag Overlyric
  from Applications to the Bin. No hard feelings.

The lyrics are community-made, so now and then a song won't have any.
That's your cue to make up your own.

Happy singing!
